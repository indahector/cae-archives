Wind speed

Characterization of the cadcat 10 m wind speed

Published

November 12, 2026

Purpose. Characterize Cal-Adapt Analytics Engine 10 m wind speed (wspeed / wspd10mean), 1980–2014, against the quality dimensions fixed in Scope & Definitions. Findings derived here feed the good/bad matrix.

Evaluated against station observations from HDP first, then against CONUS404 and ERA5.

TipHeadline

The products are right where we can check them, and can be wrong in a way the mean does not show.

At flat, open airport stations, the exposure class closest to what a grid cell represents, both products are unbiased: LOCA2 0.996, WRF 0.995 across 207 stations.

But the distribution is too narrow. Observed Weibull shape k is 1.51; the products give 2.18 and 2.92. At those same stations both under-predict wind power density by 13–19 %, and produce days above 10 m/s at 2.7 % against an observed 10.5 %, one quarter of the observed rate.

ImportantCompare against observations first

The first obvious way that comes to mind when reviewing a gridded product is to compare it against a reanalysis. The ratio between AE’s WRF/LOCA2 and ERA5 is 1.5–1.6 over land, which reads as a 60 % error, and is not one.

ERA5 at 0.25° (~31 km) cannot represent a 3 km ridge–valley contrast, so over terrain the two are measuring different things and neither is wrong for disagreeing. §7 uses ERA5 as a common grid and a diagnostic, not as truth.

1. What the stations can and cannot be

Stations are the primary reference, not a common frame in the gridding sense, and the difference matters:

  • They are points. A station samples one spot; a LOCA2/WRF cell averages 9 km² and an ERA5 cell ~950 km². Part of every model–station difference is this footprint mismatch and cannot be removed.
  • They do not cover the ocean, and they are sparse above 2,000 m, exactly where §7 shows the products and ERA5 diverge most.

So a gridded comparison still earns its place, for spatial and seasonal structure and for the ocean control. That is what §7 uses ERA5 for.

Screening

338 of 1,207 stations were removed, and the breakdown matters for how the archive is described:

reason n
record < 60 months 292
< 20 distinct values (stuck sensor) 98
observed mean < 0.5 m/s 46
excluded prefix RAWS_TR 73

869 stations retained.

The observed distribution is not remotely Gaussian

Pooled over 108 million valid hourly observations: mean 2.47 m/s, median 2.00, p90 5.37, p99 10.20.

13.2 % of values are exactly zero and 38.3 % fall below 1.5 m/s. The zero spike is the ASOS calm floor, reports below roughly 3 kt are recorded as 0 and No model can reproduce it. Every model/observation ratio in this page is therefore slightly generous to the model.

2. Level at station locations

At airports, agreement between both products and observations is remarkable: Median model / observation of 0.996 (LOCA2) and 0.995 (WRF) across 207 flat, open stations. Two entirely different downscaling methods land within half a percent of observations and of each other.

Unbiased is not accurate: Only 33 % of airport stations fall within ±10 % for WRF (48 % for LOCA2); the IQRs are 0.84–1.15 and 0.88–1.10. The errors cancel across 200 stations rather than being individually small, the signature of point-versus-area sampling. For a user siting a single project, the ±10 % figure is very relevant.

Every other station class reads low relative to the products.

class n observed mean LOCA2 WRF
airport 207 2.90 m/s 0.996 0.995
vegetated (RAWS) 469 2.41 m/s 1.171 1.517
irrigated (CIMIS) 173 1.96 m/s 1.403 1.488

Airports record more wind than any other class despite sitting lowest: median 135 m against RAWS’s 1,094 m. RAWS masts are often on ridges but exist to represent wildland fire danger, fuel moisture and ecological conditions, so they sit in vegetation-representative clearings; canopy shelter dominates.

LOCA2 and WRF diverge sharply at sheltered sites: 1.17 against 1.52 at RAWS, while being identical at airports. LOCA2 is smoother over complex terrain, produces less terrain-driven acceleration, and so lands nearer observations that are themselves sheltered.

2.1 Does a product explain the variation between sites?

Both products explain between-site variation far better than ERA5.

network ERA5 LOCA2 WRF
ASOSAWOS 0.321 0.668 0.681
OtherISD 0.391 0.670 0.518
CIMIS 0.065 0.571 0.656
RAWS 0.294 0.799 0.444

ERA5’s std_ratio of 0.495 is the resolution deficit made visible at points. A 31 km field carries only half the station-to-station variance that exists; both km-scale-informed products recover most of it (0.86 and 0.92). The km-scale products carry real point-scale information that ERA5 does not.

3. Between products, at station locations

The two WRF drivers are interchangeable, which is what licenses treating them as one product: median difference +0.60 %, IQR −0.76 % to +1.65 %. The driver changes the weather, not the climate.

The products differ depending on where they are measured:

all stations airport irrigated
LOCA2 1.166 0.996 1.403
WRF 1.354 0.995 1.488

The pooled column is the least useful number in the table. LOCA2 1.166 against WRF 1.354 looks like a substantive difference between products, but it is largely a difference in how each responds to stations sheltered by amounts neither model knows. §4 asks the same question without that confusion.

4. CONUS404: do independent km-scale models agree?

On CONUS404’s own 4 km grid, with no coarse field involved:

product median ratio n_cells
WRF 1.020 225,848
LOCA2 0.902 49,773

Two independently built km-scale models agree to 2 %. Different institutions, different configurations, different land-surface and physics choices.

LOCA2 at 0.902 sits 10 % below an independent km-scale model while sitting 29 % above ERA5 (§7). Statistical downscaling inherits the smoother field it was trained to reproduce and does not manufacture the acceleration a dynamical model generates.

Taken with §2 the picture is consistent. At airports both products are right. Away from airports LOCA2 is lower than WRF; against an unobstructed km-scale comparator LOCA2 is lower than WRF.

5. Distribution shape and extremes

This is where both products have issues with respect to the observations, and monthly means hide it completely.

Weibull k describes the balance between calm and windy days; lower k means a broader distribution.

source mean k (shape) A (scale) power W/m²
stations 2.43 1.51 2.69 23.7
LOCA2 2.94 2.18 3.32 27.4
WRF 3.40 2.92 3.81 34.3
ERA5 2.16 2.32 2.44 10.3

Observed k is 1.51; the products give 2.18 and 2.92, too few calm days and too few windy ones.

At airports, where the level is right, both under-predict power density:

source speed ratio power ratio
LOCA2 0.977 0.812
WRF 1.077 0.875
ERA5 0.757 0.355

A product matching observed mean wind to within 8 % delivers 13–19 % less power density, the number a resource assessment or calculation will inherit.

Upper tail

group mean ratio q90 ratio q99 ratio
all stations 1.124 0.974 0.867
airports 0.835 0.786 0.782

The ratios fall monotonically with quantile. A scale-factor error would keep them flat. Falling ratios mean the modelled distribution is too narrow at the top, agreeing with the Weibull shape above, arrived at independently from daily maxima.

6. Seasonal and regional structure

  • The seasonal cycle of agreement is resolved comparably across regions.
  • Both products agree slightly better in summer than in winter.
  • LOCA2 peaks in January almost everywhere (6 of 8 regions) while WRF’s peak month scatters across October, November, December, January and May, a statistical method inheriting one domain-wide seasonal signature, and a dynamical model producing region-specific seasonality.

6.1 Regional structure

Agreement is regionally uniform at airports and regionally structured everywhere else.

region n_air LOCA2 WRF
North Coast 12 1.122 1.050
Bay & Delta 25 1.046 0.811
Central Coast 8 0.983 0.917
South Coast 60 1.052 0.952
Sierra Nevada 11 1.018 1.106
Central Valley 25 0.996 1.064
Mojave/Sonoran 46 0.873 1.020
Northeast 10 0.827 1.110

Most regions sit within ±10 % of 1.0 for both products. The domain-wide airport result of §2 is not an average of large opposing regional errors; it holds region by region.

The exceptions are informative and not shared. LOCA2 runs low in the Mojave (0.873) and Northeast (0.827). The two most arid, thermally driven regions, where WRF is fine (1.020, 1.110). WRF runs low in the Bay & Delta (0.811), the one region whose wind climate is dominated by a sea breeze through a single topographic feature. Each product fails in a regime the other handles.

7. ERA5: where and when coarse and fine fields diverge

Used as a common grid and a diagnostic, not as truth, and for the one thing it is uniquely good for: complete uniform coverage, which supplies a control region where the mechanism under test should switch off.

Over open water the km-scale models and ERA5 agree to within half a percent. Over land they diverge by 52–62 %.

product ocean coastal land pooled
WRF 0.995 1.167 1.617 1.458
CONUS404 0.996 1.180 1.522 1.124
LOCA2 — 1.163 1.289 1.285

The ocean column is a positive control and it is decisive. WRF 0.995 and CONUS404 0.996. Two independently configured 3–4 km models agreeing with a 31 km reanalysis to within 0.5 % where there is no sub-grid terrain to resolve.

A calibration error would bias a model everywhere, including over water. It does not. So the land disagreement is a resolution difference, not an error.

8. What this means for the matrix

dimension LOCA2 WRF
mean bias ✅ 0.996 at airports ✅ 0.995 at airports
spatial pattern ⚠️ too smooth (0.902 vs CONUS404) ✅ 1.020 vs CONUS404
terrain response ⚠️ flat across elevation bands ✅ tracks sub-grid relief
seasonal structure ✅ flat ratio, one domain-wide peak ✅ flat ratio, regional peaks
diurnal structure ❓ no hourly data ❓ — hourly analysis not done
extremes ❓ no max-wind variable ⚠️ tail 13 % short at q99
distribution shape ⚠️ k = 2.18 vs 1.51 ⚠️ k = 2.92 vs 1.51

The pattern across the row is the finding. Both products pass on level and fail on shape. The use cases users actually bring would determine whether this limitation is critical.

9. Caveats

On the observational reference

  1. Only airports are unobstructed. Every other class confounds model error with shelter (§2)..
  2. Footprint. A station is a point; a LOCA2 cell averages 9 km² and an ERA5 cell ~950 km². Part of §2’s “only a third within ±10 %” is this and cannot be removed.
  3. A calm floor. 13.2 % of hourly observations are exactly zero, the ASOS/AWOS sub-3-kt convention. No model reproduces it, so observed means are biased low and every ratio here is slightly generous to the model.
  4. Reporting quantisation. Harmless for means; relevant to §5’s Weibull fits and quantile ratios.
  5. 338 of 1,207 stations screened out, 292 for records under 60 months. The retained 869 are not a random sample of the archive.
  6. Coverage gaps. No ocean stations, and few unobstructed high-terrain stations, the Sierra has 11 airports against 73 RAWS. The products are least verifiable exactly where §7 shows they diverge most from ERA5.

On what is not measured

  1. No uncertainty. Every result is a median with no bootstrap, no confidence interval, no effective sample size for autocorrelated series.
  2. No hourly data in this analysis, so no diurnal analysis. It is not settled how valid a model-against-hourly-station comparison would be.
  3. Historical only. No check that skill is stationary under warming.

Worth doing next

  1. Bootstrap everything: every headline number is a point estimate.
  2. Diagnose the distribution shape error : is it effective resolution, temporal averaging, or the PBL scheme?
  3. Analyse hourly wind.
