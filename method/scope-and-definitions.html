Scope & Definitions

Applied to every variable

Published

November 12, 2026

Purpose. Set the boundaries and the method before characterization starts, so results are comparable across variables and the work is repeatable. Items marked [CONFIRM in review] / [FILL] are decisions for the science team; everything else is drawn from the lit-review database and flagged with source IDs.

1. Datasets in scope

The two downscaling paths treat most variables fundamentally differently, and the difference is the single most useful thing to know about them.

  • WRF 3 km CA convection-permitting (d03) : the dynamical path, and the one that physically simulates the atmosphere. Native hourly output; resolves terrain-driven and mesoscale flow. (R12, R19; R04 validation.)
  • LOCA2 CA hybrid, 3 km : the statistical/hybrid downscaling. Trained to reproduce an observational target, so it inherits that target’s spatial structure rather than generating its own.

[CONFIRM in review] exact cadcat versions/paths, and whether 9 km WUS-D3 is in scope.

Not every variable is downscaled at parity. Wind is not statistically downscaled in LOCA2 the way temperature and precipitation are (R02).

2. Extent

  • Spatial: California 3 km domain (d03).
  • Temporal: 1980–2100; SSP3-7.0 (other SSPs?) [CONFIRM with review].

  • Vertical: surface.

4. The reference hierarchy

Three tiers, ordered by what each can settle. The order is part of the method.

tier example answers cannot answer
point observations HDP station network Is the product right where we measured? ocean; unobstructed high terrain; anywhere unsampled
independent fine-scale model CONUS404 (4 km, NCAR/USGS) Do independent models agree? whether either is right
reanalysis ERA5 (0.25°) Where and when do coarse and fine diverge? finer scale structures

Observations first. A ratio against a coarse reanalysis measures a distance, not an error: at 0.25° (~31 km) a reanalysis cannot represent a 3 km ridge–valley contrast, so disagreement over terrain is expected and is not by itself evidence against either side.

The reanalysis earns its place through coverage. It is the only field that is complete and uniform, which makes it the only one that can supply a control region, somewhere the mechanism under test should switch off. For wind that is the ocean, where a resolution effect must vanish and a calibration error must not.

Station siting is the limiting factor, and it is not standardised. Instrument height usually is. Where the instrument stands is not, and that difference is a large source of scatter in every station comparison. Open (exposed) sites will behave differently from sheltered ones.

5. Choosing the metric

The metric is not universal. Decide it before any comparison is run and state it on the characterization page. Wind speed is an unbounded variable, while relative humidity is bounded between 0 and 100 %.

A ratio is meaningless for a bounded variable: where observed RH is 90 %, no model can score above 1.11 however wrong it is, so a map of RH ratio is largely a map of where the observations are high.

Beyond a bias, two diagnostics have proved decisive and are now standard:

Distribution analysis examines the overall shape of the variable’s distribution, and a quantile comparison (Q–Q against observations). A straight line through the origin means the departure is affine, a shift and a stretch, and a simple correction is the right functional form. Curvature means it is not.

7. Known issues to carry in from the start

  • Absolute bias and its sensitivity to model configuration.
  • Complex terrain and coastal transitions, where both model error and observational reference are worst (R18).
  • The sparse-California-reference problem for anything trained on observations (DQ9; R16).
  • Robustness of projected changes for maxima vs means (DQ6); spatial consistency of trends across GWLs (DQ7).
