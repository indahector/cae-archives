R01 — Is Bias Correction in Dynamical Downscaling Defensible?

Risser et al. 2024 (GRL 51, e2023GL105979) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 4

Summary. Tests whether a priori bias correction (APBC) of GCM boundary conditions before dynamical downscaling is justified.

Key findings. APBC adds negligible uncertainty for temperature and precipitation relative to GCM choice and internal variability. Snow projections without APBC are physically unrealistic. Minimally invasive APBC preserves GCM trends at regional scales.

Datasets: WRF (WUS-D3) · Variables: Temperature, precipitation, snow · Methods: A priori bias correction of boundary conditions; comparison of uncertainty sources

Feeds: Phase 2 proposal; Sec 4 methods shortlist; provenance flowchart (pre-downscaling branch)

Relevance: High · Status: To read

Core justification for the APBC step already applied upstream of AE WRF data

R02 — LOCA Version 2 for North America (ca. Jan 2023)

Pierce / Scripps (UCSD) 2023 · Dataset documentation · Sec 0

Summary. Official documentation page for the LOCA2 downscaled dataset.

Key findings. LOCA2 CMIP6 at 6 km, daily Tmin/Tmax/precip 1950-2100, SSP245/370/585, 27 GCMs / 329 members. Humidity in progress. Wind is not statistically downscaled - supports the LOCA2-barely-carries-wind caveat.

Datasets: LOCA2 (NAmer 6 km) · Variables: Tmax, Tmin, precipitation · Methods: LOCA2 statistical downscaling and its bias correction step

Feeds: Per-dataset bias adjustment documentation; variable cards

Relevance: High · Status: To read

Methodology papers are listed in the LOCA bibliography page - chase and add as separate rows

R05 — Observed humidity trends in dry regions contradict climate models

Simpson et al. 2024 (PNAS 121(1), e2302480120) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 1

Summary. Documents the mismatch between observed and modelled humidity trends in arid regions.

Key findings. CMIP6 models fail to reproduce observed drying (negative RH / vapor pressure trends) in arid and semi-arid regions including the SW US. ERA5 shows a much stronger negative RH trend than the GCMs. This is the root paper behind the AE RH concern.

Datasets: CMIP6 GCMs, ERA5 · Variables: RH, vapor pressure · Methods: Linear trend fitting on observed vs modelled RH

Feeds: RH report (core citation); good/bad matrix

Related questions: DQ1, DQ3

Relevance: High · Status: To read

Cited in Q02; Will reproduced its ERA5 trends in Appendix B. Citation verified Jul 2026: the article was published online 26 Dec 2023 but carries a 2 Jan 2024 issue date, so some databases (OSTI, DOE EESM) list it as 2023. The volume is 121, not 120 - the trailing 120 in the DOI is PNAS submission-era numbering, not the volume.

R06 — DRAFT - Data Justification Memo for California’s Fifth Climate Change Assessment: Climate Adjusted Synthetic Relative Humidity Data

Westerling, Baldwin, Romsos & Saah (Pyregence, draft) · Memo · Sec 4

Summary. The Pyregence team’s own justification for replacing downscaled RH with a statistically derived synthetic product, plus full model specification and data access.

Key findings. Motivation: over the 2000-2020 overlap, GCM-simulated RH is significantly wetter than observed, and observed California RH has a declining trend that the simulations do not reproduce - so fire models calibrated on observations project immediately decreasing fire activity regardless of scenario. KEY CLAIM FOR PHASE 2: the authors argue bias correction can adjust modest discrepancies in mean or variance but CANNOT correct differences in long-term trends, which is why they rebuilt RH rather than correcting it. Authors’ own stated limitations: the model omits large-scale atmospheric moisture transport, underestimates wet extremes of daily max RH, and has no dew point representation so max RH never spikes to 100%. They state the impact is muted for fire models because those are most sensitive to daily MINIMUM RH. Model skill: ~66% of variance explained for rmin, ~45% for rmax. Authors explicitly caution other sectors to assess suitability before using it as a replacement.

Datasets: Pyregence synthetic RH; source data = ERA5 WRF (1950-2021), UCLA WRF bias-corrected, LOCA2 · Variables: RH (daily min/max), tmx, tmn, prc, elevation, aspect · Methods: Semiparametric GAM via bam() in R mgcv, with spline-smoothed interaction terms. RH fractions logit-transformed to unbounded variables. Final form: rmin2 ~ s(tmx,tmn,prc) + s(t90,p90) + s(elv,asp) + s(x,y). AIC-based selection on a 1M random subsample of >1bn daily observations. Windspeed and 30/60-day leading averages were tested but not retained. A day-of-year seasonal term was deliberately excluded so seasonality would emerge from the climate data.

Feeds: Phase 2 proposal (the trend-vs-bias-correction claim is directly load-bearing); RH report; data challenges page

Related questions: DQ3, DQ5, DQ8, DQ11

Relevance: High · Status: Read

Obtained Jul 2026, resolves the placeholder. Confirms Q02‘s reading of the input variables exactly. Still does NOT name the observational training dataset, and still documents no held-out validation - ’robust across randomly selected samples’ is a stability check, not an overfitting test. Crucially it documents NO additional bias correction step on the LOCA2 or WRF-ERA5 inputs, so the large LOCA2 bias shift Q02 observed remains unexplained. Data access via ungoliant.ucmerced.edu (era5wrf/, wrf_bc/, loca2/) as Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs. Relevance raised from Medium to High.

R07 — Future humidity trends over the western United States in the CMIP5 global climate models and variable infiltration capacity hydrological modeling system

Pierce, Westerling & Oyler 2013 (HESS 17(5), 1833-1850) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 4

Summary. Prior work on deriving humidity for western US climate assessments using the VIC hydrological model.

Key findings. The methodological precedent R06 builds on and cites for its known limitation: like VIC-derived RH, a statistically/hydrologically derived humidity misses the portion of daily variability driven by large-scale atmospheric moisture transport. Establishes that this limitation is long-standing and not unique to the Pyregence approach.

Datasets: CMIP5 GCMs, VIC · Variables: RH, humidity · Methods: VIC hydrological modelling of humidity; CMIP5 humidity trend analysis

Feeds: RH report (background); Phase 2 proposal (prior art on derived-humidity limitations)

Related questions: DQ3, DQ8

Relevance: Medium · Status: To read

Chased from the R06 reference list. Pierce is also the author of R02-R04, so this connects the LOCA2 and Pyregence threads.

R08 — Statistical Downscaling Using Localized Constructed Analogs (LOCA)

Pierce, Cayan & Thrasher 2014 (J. Hydrometeorol. 15, 2558-2585) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 4

Summary. The core peer-reviewed description of the LOCA statistical downscaling method that underlies LOCA2.

Key findings. Analog-based downscaling. The LOCA system has two parts: bias correction and the downscaling step. This is the foundational method reference behind R02-R04 and the LOCA2 hybrid.

Datasets: LOCA (CMIP5 origin), applies to LOCA2 · Variables: Temperature, precipitation · Methods: Localized constructed analogs; observationally trained

Feeds: 1a per-dataset bias adjustment documentation; variable cards

Named by the LOCA bibliography as the primary description of the method.

R09 — Improved Bias Correction Techniques for Hydrological Simulations of Climate Change

Pierce et al. 2015 (J. Hydrometeorol. 16(6), 2421-2442) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 4

Summary. The peer-reviewed bias correction method used in LOCA and LOCA2 - the quantile-preserving scheme.

Key findings. This is the actual LOCA bias correction (‘Pierce et al. 2015a’ in the CEC memos): it preserves GCM-predicted trends by quantile. LOAD-BEARING FOR DQ11 - its trend-by-quantile preservation is the direct counter-case to test against the Pyregence memo’s claim that bias correction cannot fix trends.

Datasets: LOCA / LOCA2 · Variables: Temperature, precipitation · Methods: Quantile-preserving bias correction with trend preservation

Feeds: 1a documentation; Phase 2 proposal; DQ11

The standard LOCA BC scheme cited throughout the CEC memos.

R18 — Evaluation of the Mountain Hydroclimate across the Western United States in Dynamically Downscaled Climate Models

Adhikari, Geerts, Rahimi-Esfarjani, Smith, Shuman & Schneider 2024 (J. Hydrometeorol. 25(12), 1877-1894) · Peer-reviewed paper · Sec 3

Summary. Evaluates how well the WUS-D3 WRF downscaling reproduces observed hydroclimate over complex terrain in the western US.

Key findings. Evaluates 15 CMIP6 GCMs downscaled to 9 km (WUS-D3) against PRISM, gauge/station data and a snow reanalysis over 1981-2014 for orographic precipitation, surface temperature and SWE, plus ERA5-driven 9 km and 4 km (CONUS404) reconstructions. Perkins skill for precipitation exceeds 0.9 over plains but is lower over mountains. KEY FOR THE PILLAR: it flags that gridded observational estimates in complex terrain are poorly quantified (esp. PRISM precip over mountains) - the reference-data uncertainty problem that complicates bias correction and validation in exactly the terrain where wind is also hardest.

Datasets: WRF WUS-D3 (9 km); ERA5-driven WRF (9 km + CONUS404 4 km) · Variables: Orographic precipitation, surface temperature, SWE · Methods: Perkins skill score vs PRISM, gauge/station data and snow reanalysis; mountains-vs-plains contrast

Feeds: Good/bad matrix (complex-terrain rows); data challenges page; Phase 2 reference-dataset scoping

Related questions: DQ9

Relevance: Medium · Status: To read

WRF WUS-D3 evaluation, not a bias-correction method paper. Rahimi-Esfarjani = Stefan Rahimi (WUS-D3 lead). Its complex-terrain reference-uncertainty finding is the most transferable point for the wind pilot and for reference-dataset choice.

Q01 — Relative Humidity Sensitivity Analysis: Fosberg Fire Weather Index

Cal-Adapt AE 2025 (update: Nicole, Mar 2026) · Internal doc · Sec 2

Summary. AE sensitivity analysis of how the known RH bias propagates into FFWI.

Key findings. FFWI likely biased low due to the known RH bias in CMIP6 downscaled GCMs. RH trend switches sign around GWL2.5 (interior increases), making FFWI non-monotonic. Preliminary recommendation: caution or do-not-use for FFWI at GWL>=2.5. RH bias adjustment alone may not fix the FFWI trend. FFWI>50 exceedance counts trend downward over time (especially TaiESM). Mean wind speeds decrease mildly statewide.

Datasets: WRF (AE) · Variables: RH, wind speed, air temperature, FFWI · Methods: GWL delta signal analysis; FFWI decomposition by calculation step; exceedance counting

Feeds: RH report; good/bad matrix (RH and FFWI rows); FAQ

Related questions: DQ1, DQ2, DQ6, DQ7

Relevance: High · Status: Read

Open items: per-model attribution of the RH switch; whether the FFWI=50 threshold is valid for CA; max-wind (vs mean) trends

Q02 — Evaluating Pyregence Synthetic Relative Humidity Product

Cal-Adapt AE 2025 (Will Krantz) · Internal doc · Sec 3

Summary. Evaluation of whether the Pyregence synthetic RH product produces more realistic long-term RH trends.

Key findings. Verdict: acceptable only as a Pyregence wildfire-model input; unacceptable as a standalone or replacement RH product. Applies a roughly uniform negative trend adjustment but FAILS to reproduce ERA5’s strong negative trend when applied to WRF-ERA5. Undocumented training data, undocumented input bias correction, no overfitting validation. Daily-scale artifacts: blotchy spatial patterns, missing inland water, unexplained increases over southern Nevada.

R20 — Personal communication on LOCA2 CA hybrid wind speed construction

D. Pierce (Scripps), pers. comm., August 2026 [confirm exact date] · Personal communication · Sec 0

Summary. Scripps confirmation of how wspeed is constructed and bias-corrected relative to uas/vas.

Key findings. wspeed is produced by averaging hourly wind speeds to daily (scalar, positive-definite), downscaled and bias-corrected separately from the vector components (signed, can cancel), with different extreme-value handling. Therefore wspeed != sqrt(uas2+vas2) by design. Recommendation: use the downscaled wspeed directly; do not reconstruct from daily vector winds. Resolves DQ4.

Q05 — Cal-Adapt downscaled products vs ERA5: 10 m wind agreement over California (v2)

AE internal analysis (H. Inda-Diaz), 2026 · Internal analysis · Sec 1

Summary. Head-to-head validation of AE downscaled 10 m wind (LOCA2 statistical + WRF dynamical, same GCM) vs ERA5 over California, 1980-2010.

Key findings. Both products run high vs ERA5 with agreeing timing (seasonal r 0.77-0.92) and a multiplicative offset. On 1351 shared cells the STATISTICAL LOCA2 (+29%) is CLOSER to ERA5 than the DYNAMICAL WRF-GCM (+69%). This REJECTS the v1 hypothesis that LOCA2’s offset came from PresRat: the uncorrected dynamical product is the outlier and PresRat moves LOCA2 toward ERA5. Offset largest over flat interior terrain (opposite of a resolution effect), so origin is upstream (GCM wind climate, WRF surface/PBL, or ERA5 10m diagnostic at 31km). Big caveat: sampling frequency not matched (WRF native-timestep vs ERA5 hourly) inflates WRF. Decisive next step: WRF-ERA5 run (same sampling).
